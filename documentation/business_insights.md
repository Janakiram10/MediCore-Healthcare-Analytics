# MediCore — Validated Business Insights

> Synthetic portfolio data. These are analytical observations, not real healthcare benchmarks.

## Executive
- 500,000 appointments and 300,000 encounters were analyzed.
- Total billed amount: ₹178.13 Cr.
- Total collected amount: ₹111.98 Cr.
- Outstanding amount: ₹33.01 Cr.
- Overall collection rate: 62.86%.
- Overall no-show rate on the validated eligible appointment base: 9.81%.
- Average patient satisfaction score: 74.61.
- Average wait time: 62.18 minutes.
- 30-day readmission rate: 3.65%.

## No-show
- Overall appointment mix: 83.19% completed, 9.05% no-show, 7.75% cancelled.
- Longer lead times were associated with higher raw no-show rates; 31+ days had 13.74% versus 9.31% for same-day bookings.
- Booking-channel rates varied, with Online at 10.36% and Referral at 7.47% in the raw comparison.
- Multivariable logistic regression had limited explanatory power (pseudo R² ≈ 0.0078), so the analysis should be treated as risk segmentation rather than a strong predictive model.

## Revenue
- H005 had the highest encounter volume and highest absolute outstanding exposure.
- Hospital collection rates were tightly clustered around 62.52%–63.19%, so absolute outstanding exposure is more actionable than small rate differences.
- Uninsured encounters had the highest absolute outstanding exposure at about ₹5.17 Cr.
- HealthSecure Basic had the lowest observed collection rate at 44.58%.
- Inpatient care generated the largest billed amount and outstanding balance, but collection rates across inpatient, outpatient, and emergency were all close to 63%; encounter type alone was not a strong differentiator.

## Operations and patient experience
- H007 had the highest average wait time at 66.18 minutes despite lower volume.
- General Medicine had the highest department-level wait time at about 69.04 minutes.
- Consultation duration was relatively stable across hospitals.
- Satisfaction declined consistently as wait time increased: 79.51 for <45 minutes versus 65.89 for 75+ minutes.
- Complaint rate rose from 5.75% in the <45-minute group to 11.87% for 75+ minutes.

## Readmission
- 66,900 admissions were evaluated; 2,440 were readmissions within 30 days, producing a 3.65% rate.
- Urinary Infection and Hypertension were among the higher observed diagnosis-level rates (4.07% and 3.99%).
- Diagnosis-level variation was modest, so these groups should be monitored rather than described as proven causal drivers.

## Decision guidance
Prioritize high-volume hospitals/departments with high absolute outstanding exposure and long waits, then monitor payer-level collection performance and diagnosis groups with elevated readmission rates.
